After about 40, many people start holding their phone or newspaper further away to read. This is presbyopia: the natural lens inside the eye slowly becomes stiffer, so focusing up close gets harder. It happens to almost everyone and is not a disease. Common signs are holding things at arm's length, tired eyes or headaches after reading, and needing brighter light. VIDEO: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Vijay-Tamil/lenzkare-social/main/2026-12/yt/2026-12-09-astigmatism-explained.mp4

▶ YOUTUBE (Short)
Title: Astigmatism explained: the rugby-ball eye 🏉 #Shorts
Description:
Street lights look stretched at night? It may be astigmatism.
இரவில் விளக்குகள் நீண்டு தெரிகிறதா? இது அஸ்டிக்மாடிசமாக இருக்கலாம்.

• A normal cornea is round like a football; with astigmatism it curves more one way, like a rugby ball
• Light focuses at two points instead of one, so near and far can both blur
• Signs: squinting, eye strain after screens, headaches, glare or streaks around lights at night
• It is very common and often comes along with short or long sight
• Glasses with a 'cylinder' (CYL) power correct it; toric contact lenses are another option
• Power changing quickly or sudden drop in vision? See an eye specialist

Astigmatism is one of the most common reasons for blurry vision. The cornea, the clear front window of the eye, is normally round like a football. With astigmatism it is curved more in one direction, like a rugby ball, so light focuses at two points instead of one. Things can look blurry or stretched at any distance, and lights may streak or glare at night. Other signs are squinting, eye strain after screen work and headaches. It is not a disease and often comes together with short or long sight. Glasses with a cylinder (CYL) power correct it, and toric contact lenses are another option.
