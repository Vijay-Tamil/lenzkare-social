• The clear lens behind the pupil slowly turns cloudy, mostly with age; WHO lists cataract as the leading cause of blindness worldwide, yet it is treatable.
• Signs: dazzling glare at night, hazy vision like dirty glasses, faded colours, needing more light, frequent power changes.
• Diabetes, smoking, strong sun and eye injury can bring it sooner; UV-protective sunglasses help.
• Myths: you don't need to wait for it to 'ripen', and no drops or diet can dissolve a cataract.
• Surgery replaces the cloudy lens with a clear artificial lens; most people return to light daily activities within days, as their surgeon advises.
• Most people still need glasses after surgery, especially for reading; get your new power checked once the eye has settled.
• Sudden vision loss, a painful red eye, or flashes with a curtain: see an eye specialist the same day.
