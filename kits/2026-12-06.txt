• Babies are born with blurry vision; the eyes and brain learn to see together over the first months and years.
• Milestones: following faces by about 3 months, reaching for toys and judging distance in the first year, enjoying picture books by toddler age.
• Myopia (short sight) in children is rising worldwide; signs include sitting close to the TV, squinting and headaches.
• Daily outdoor play in daylight helps lower the risk of myopia; keep screens at a distance with regular breaks.
• Squint is not 'lucky eyes', and a lazy eye responds best when treated in early childhood.
• Check eyes at birth, before school (3–5 years) and every year after.
• See an eye specialist soon if eyes don't follow your face by 3 months, an eye turns, or you notice a white glow in the pupil in photos.

Title: Smoking hurts your eyes too: 3 facts 🚭 #Shorts
Description:
Smoking doesn't just hurt your lungs. 3 eye facts every smoker should know.
புகைபிடித்தல் கண்களையும் பாதிக்கும்.

• Smoke irritates and dries the surface of the eye, even for people nearby.
• Studies show smokers are more likely to get cataracts (the eye's clear lens turning cloudy).
• Smoking raises the risk of age-related macular degeneration (AMD), which damages central vision used for reading and faces.
• Why: smoke harms tiny blood vessels and puts extra stress on delicate eye tissue.
• Good news: quitting lowers your risk at any age. Walk daily, eat leafy greens and colourful vegetables.
• Keep BP and sugar in check; get a yearly eye test after 40.
• See an eye specialist soon if straight lines look wavy or a dark spot appears in the centre of your vision.
